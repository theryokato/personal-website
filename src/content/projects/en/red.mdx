## Overview
While Elysium 1.1 delivered major gains through small, targeted improvements, Elysium 2 represents RED’s next
historic step. This engine introduces an entirely new architecture that will become the most powerful rocket
engine ever fired at Texas A&M. With built-in throttling and gimbaling capabilities, Elysium 2 provides the
platform RED needs to develop more advanced, flight-relevant systems and push the program into a new tier of
technical complexity.

### Flame Diverter
During my time at Rocket Engine Design (RED), I designed the flame diverter for the E2 hotfire test stand. The diverter was designed to redirect the hot exhaust away from the test stand in a controllable manner, while also being able to withstand the extreme heat and pressure of the rocket engine exhaust.
- Stacked 0.5" thick 304 Stainless Steel plates, bended
- 3/4" Carbon Steel square rails (welded, 18ft total)
- 3/8" Grade 5 Steel Bolts & Nuts
- 26" aluminum penetrators
- 45&deg; and 10&deg; angles
- 31" x 16.5" x 18" (L x W x H)

### Project Ragnarok
Project Ragnarok was a project within RED that aims to modify the Elysium 2 engine into a regeneratively cooled engine. As a member of the avionics subteam, I oversaw detailed component analysis for electronics, wires, and sensors to confirm compatibility and prevent integration issues.
